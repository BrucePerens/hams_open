Getting Help: Support Tickets

If something on hams.com is not working, you have a question, or you need to tell us about a problem, open a support ticket. A ticket is a written request that stays on your account, so you can see what was said and what state it is in. You must be signed in to use it.

Where to Find Your Tickets

Click Support in the site menu. It is shown only when you are signed in. You can also reach the same list from your account page, on the card named Helpdesk Tickets.

The list shows your tickets, newest first, twenty to a page. For each ticket you see:

  • Ticket # and Title: click either one to open the ticket.
  • Callsign: the callsign on the ticket.
  • Priority: Low, Medium, High or Critical.
  • Stage: where the ticket stands (see "What the Statuses Mean" below).

If you have not opened a ticket yet, the page says there are no tickets for your account.

The list shows tickets from the site you are using, plus tickets that are not tied to any one site, such as ones that began as an e-mail.

Opening a Ticket

  1. Click Support, then the New Ticket button.
  2. Fill in Subject. This is the only box you must fill in. If you leave it empty, you are taken back to the form.
  3. Check Callsign. It starts out filled in from your account if you have one. Leave it empty if you do not have a callsign, for example if you are studying for your license.
  4. Pick a Category (see below). The default is General Inquiry.
  5. Write what happened in Description.
  6. Click Submit Ticket. Your new ticket opens right away.

What to Put in the Ticket

A short, clear subject and a complete description save everyone a round of questions. Helpful things to include:

  • What you were trying to do, and what happened instead.
  • The steps you took, in order, so someone else can try the same thing.
  • The exact wording of any error message.
  • The page address, the callsign involved, and roughly when it happened (with your time zone).
  • Your device and browser, if the problem looks like a display or button problem.

Do not put passwords in a ticket.

The New Ticket form has no place to attach a file. Describe what you see in words.

Choosing a Category

The Category list tells us what kind of problem this is. For most questions, leave it on General Inquiry.

  • General Inquiry: questions and problems of any kind.
  • hams_local_relay: Bug / Security Report: for a bug or a security problem in the hams_local_relay program. Say in the description which of the two it is. Links from the relay guide open the form with this category already chosen.

Depending on which features are turned on at the site, the list can have other choices too, such as a silent key report, an event correction, a repeater ownership report, or a Simulated Band report. Those exist mostly for the Report buttons described below. If you want to report something about an event, a repeater or the Simulated Band, use the button on that page instead of the general form. The button carries along what the general form cannot, such as which listing you mean.

If you are not sure, choose General Inquiry. We can sort it out.

Following a Ticket

Open a ticket from the list to see:

  • Its subject, as the page heading.
  • The Status, your Callsign if there is one, and the Priority.
  • The description you wrote.
  • The Communication History: the messages on the ticket, with a box underneath for writing a message of your own.

To add information or answer a question, write in the box under Communication History and send it. Come back to the ticket from Support whenever you like.

You choose neither the priority nor the status. The people handling tickets set them.

Tickets you open on the site start at Low priority. Staff can change it.

What the Statuses Mean

  • New: the ticket has been received and no one has started on it yet.
  • In Progress: someone is working on it.
  • Resolved: we believe the problem is dealt with. You can still read the ticket and close it yourself.
  • Closed: the ticket is finished.
  • Spam / Phishing (Quarantined): an e-mail that our mail check suspected of being unwanted or fraudulent. If you wrote to us in good faith and see this on your ticket, a person can move it back to New.

Closing Your Ticket

When your problem is solved, or you no longer need help, open the ticket and click Close Ticket. The ticket moves to Closed and a note, "Ticket closed by customer," is added to Communication History. Once a ticket is closed the button is no longer shown, so there is no button to reopen it. If the same problem comes back, open a new ticket and mention the old ticket number.

E-mail and Notifications

When you open a ticket, you are signed up for updates on it. Every time the status of your ticket changes, you get an e-mail whose subject starts "Ticket Update:" and whose message says your issue has been updated and gives the new status. Closing the ticket yourself also sends one. The only exception is the Spam / Phishing (Quarantined) status, which sends nothing.

You can also open a ticket by e-mail. A message sent to support@hams.com or admin@hams.com becomes a ticket. If the address you wrote from matches your hams.com account, the ticket is linked to your account and appears under Support. If it does not match, the ticket exists but will not appear in your list, so write from the address on your account.

E-mailed tickets start at Medium priority.

Report Buttons Elsewhere on the Site

A few pages have their own Report button that sends your report to the support team as a ticket. Not every Report button on the site does this, so each is described here.

Simulated Band: Report Misconduct

The Simulated Band console always shows a Report Misconduct button in its status line. It opens a form titled "Report misconduct on the Simulated Band", and nothing is sent until you click Send report.

  1. Under "What happened?", choose one: Obscene or offensive language, Harassment, A child may be at risk or in danger, or Something else.
  2. Under "Which station?", pick the station you mean, or say you are not sure.
  3. Optionally, describe what you heard or saw.
  4. Click Send report.

The form tells you that recordings of the band are kept for review, and that the report includes the last ten minutes of what your console heard (decodes and captions), the frequency and the times, and your operator details.

You can report even if you are only listening. A report about a child at risk is marked as the most urgent kind. When the report is received, the form shows a ticket reference. Write down this reference. A Simulated Band report is not tied to your account, so it does not show up in your Support list. If you report too many times in a short while, the form asks you to wait before sending another.

Repeater Directory: Report Improper Ownership

When you are signed in, listings in the repeater directory that are already owned show a Report improper ownership button. It opens a box titled "Report Improper Ownership". Explain in Details why you believe the ownership is wrong and, if you run the machine, how we can confirm it, then click File Report. The directory does not show you who currently holds the listing.

You land on your new ticket, which is also in your Support list. You can have only one open report for the same listing at a time, and an account that has been flagged for misusing directory listings cannot file one.

The separate "Report a Hijacked or Inaccurate Listing" form on the same page is a simple accuracy note and does not create a Support ticket.

Events: Report an Issue to the Event Owner

On an event page, Report an Issue to the Event Owner sends your note to the person who owns or has adopted the event. Only when an event has no real person to tell does your note become a support ticket for the support team instead. If one is already open for that event, your note is added to it. If you were signed in when you reported, the ticket is linked to your account. Because several people can add to the same ticket, it may not appear in your own list.

The Report Abuse to the System Administrator button on an event page goes to the administrators and does not open a Support ticket.

What Happens After You Submit

  1. Your ticket is created at once and you are taken to it. It starts in New.
  2. If an administrator is on duty, the ticket is assigned to them. If no one is on duty, it waits unassigned until someone picks it up.
  3. Staff may write back in Communication History, ask for more detail, and move the ticket along the statuses above. Each status change e-mails you.
  4. When it is finished, the ticket reaches Resolved or Closed. You can close it yourself at any time.

This manual does not promise a reply time, because none is set in the system. For something urgent, say so plainly in the subject and the description.

Who Can See Your Tickets

  • Other members cannot see your tickets. The Support list and the ticket page only show tickets that belong to your own account, and asking for anyone else's ticket sends you back to your account page.
  • The people who handle support can read and answer tickets so they can help you.
  • Whatever you write in a ticket becomes part of its record, so leave out anything you do not want kept with it, such as passwords or payment details.
