Form Shield: spam protection that does not track you

Forms that anyone can send (reporting a page, asking for an invitation, contacting us, signing up for a newsletter) attract programs that fill them in. The form shield stops most of them without a CAPTCHA picture and without any company's tracking script.

For visitors

While a form is open, your browser does a small calculation in the background. It takes a moment, it needs no clicks, and the form tells you ("Verifying you are human…") so a screen reader announces it. You can keep typing. If you press Send early, the form waits for the calculation and then sends.

If your browser cannot do the calculation, a link on the form leads to a page with one question in words, such as "What is seven plus four?". Type the answer as digits.

If you see a message that you sent too many forms, wait an hour. If a message says the check timed out, open the form again and resend.

What is kept

  • Briefly, a one-way code made from the first part of your network address (never the address itself), used only to limit how many forms one network can send.
  • A counter per day, per form and per reason, of submissions that were turned away. It holds no address, email or text.

For administrators

Settings, then Form shield counters, shows how many submissions each form accepted and turned away, and why (honeypot, replay, rate limit and so on). A sudden rise in one reason tells you what the spammers are trying. The limits and the difficulty are system parameters beginning with form_shield.; see the module README.

Developers: a controller asks the form shield model to check the posted form (check_form, with the form's id and the posted values); a template includes form_shield.fields inside a form marked with data-form-shield.
